# Retaining preflop training information

24 September 2026. The completed exact-initial pilot loses substantial root
sample information through its shared reservoir. A separate small accumulator
is implemented and has passed replay, native training/restart and CPU/CUDA
policy controls. It is research-only; improved poker strength is unproven.

## Evidence from the completed run

Root samples start being discarded at update 21. At the last played model,
generation 77, only 12,117 of 39,424 root samples remain (30.74%). All 169 hand
classes still have observations, so this is loss of information within classes,
not missing classes. Before replacement starts, the full-sample and retained
means agree to roundoff.

At that same generation boundary, recomputing the root policy from all recorded
samples gives these aggregate frequencies:

| Action | Actual retained-sample model | Full-sample diagnostic |
|---|---:|---:|
| Fold | 43.59% | 41.55% |
| Call | 35.68% | 43.12% |
| Raise | 20.29% | 14.65% |
| Jam | 0.43% | 0.68% |

The incoming-mass-weighted total variation is 0.33865: at the hand level the
readouts differ substantially, beyond what the aggregate totals show. This
is a single played generation, not the complete averaged candidate. The
diagnostic does not change the model or evaluate these alternative decisions.

Generation 78, which is unplayed and excluded from the primary candidate,
retains 12,136 of 39,936 root samples. Full per-class counts range from 95 to
421; retained counts range from 25 to 139. Independent scalar summation agrees
with the reconstructed means to 5.33e-14 bb. These are recorded sampled
advantages, not exact action values.

Keeping all samples' sums and counts requires only 6,760 bytes of numeric
state for this 169-class, four-action root. Network training and the original
shared reservoir can remain unchanged. This avoids simply allocating an
ever-larger reservoir for every postflop observation.

## What this establishes and does not establish

The old reservoir is a valid uniform sample; discarding entries is intentional,
not a parsing bug. It adds avoidable sampling variability at a small preflop
decision where complete cumulative totals are affordable. Call/raise targets
still have substantial sampling noise even when every target is kept.

The full-sample diagnostic reuses targets generated by the old policies.
Training with full retention changes later policies and their targets, so
these diagnostic frequencies cannot be installed as a repaired strategy.
Nor do they establish how much of the measured 0.23038 bb weakness will vanish.
A fresh training comparison and independent test are necessary.

## Completed implementation controls

- All 78 recorded updates and 39,936 root samples replayed through the new
  accumulator. Independent scalar sums agree to 2.10e-11; resulting
  probabilities agree to 5.22e-15.
- Serialized state was restored at every boundary. Eleven malformed update,
  state and query cases were rejected, including incomplete or duplicate
  batches, mixed policies and unsupported legacy table readers.
- The root override leaves 28,994 non-root query rows unchanged in the replay
  fixture. Runtime bank averaging applies it before computing own-action reach.
- Two fresh tiny joint updates passed on CPU and CUDA. Restoring update one
  and replaying update two reproduces models, raw native artifacts, both
  accumulators, reservoirs and random streams on each backend.
- Thirty-six complete-bank CPU/CUDA comparisons, with independent direct
  own-history products, passed. Maximum probability difference was 8.00e-15;
  maximum reach difference was 2.00e-14. Eight single-model comparisons passed.

The model/checkpoint envelope is explicitly version 5. Its nested version 4
state preserves the original retained tables for audit; it cannot silently
load as the new policy in an older reader. Production 56708 is unchanged.
